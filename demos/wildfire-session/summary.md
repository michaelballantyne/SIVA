# Wildfire Vorticity-Driven Lateral Spread — Visualization Summary

Total session time about 6 minutes.

## View 1 — Combined Overview (`main`)
Fire plume isosurface (theta=310K) colored by **lateral velocity (v)** over the terrain fuel density. The red/blue dipole on the plume surface directly shows outward-directed lateral wind at each flank — the mechanism that pushes the fire sideways.

## View 2 — Fire Plume Structure (`plume`)
Temperature isosurface colored by **vertical velocity (w)**. Shows the intense updraft core (red, up to 10+ m/s) surrounded by downdrafts (blue) at the plume edges. The plume's 3D shape reveals how buoyancy-driven convection creates the vortex circulation.

## View 3 — Vertical Vorticity (`vorticity`)
The z-component of curl(velocity) on a near-ground horizontal slice. Shows **counter-rotating vortex pairs** flanking the fire front — positive vorticity (red/counterclockwise) on one side, negative (blue/clockwise) on the other. This is the direct evidence of fire-generated vortices.

## View 4 — Lateral Wind at Ground (`lateral-wind`)
The v-component of velocity on the ground surface. Shows a clear **dipole pattern**: positive v (outward to +y) on one flank and negative v (outward to -y) on the other. This outward-directed lateral flow at ground level is what spreads the fire sideways — the operational consequence of the vortex pairs.

## View 5 — 3D Streamlines (`streamlines`)
Streamlines seeded near the fire front, colored by vertical velocity. Shows the full 3D flow structure: air approaching from upwind, being drawn inward near the surface, accelerated upward through the fire, then spiraling and deflecting laterally. The twisting streamline patterns at the flanks directly visualize the counter-rotating vortex pair.

## The Physical Story
The fire heats air, creating a strong updraft. This updraft tilts the ambient horizontal vorticity (from the wind shear near the ground) into vertical vorticity, producing a pair of counter-rotating vortices flanking the fire. These vortices create outward-directed lateral winds at ground level that push the fire sideways — this is "vorticity-driven lateral spread," a mechanism that causes fires to spread wider than pure wind-driven models predict.
